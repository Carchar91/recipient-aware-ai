# Dopamine Hooks

Make relevance visible and deliver meaningful closure without fake urgency or neurochemical claims.

## Use and do not use
Use for a headline, opening or passage that needs a clear reason to read and a delivered answer. “Dopamine Hooks” is the original working name. The functional scope is Attention & Closure, not dopamine measurement, neuroscience or addictive engagement design.

## Inputs
Read the actual message, audience task, supported claims and what the material can genuinely explain. A hook cannot promise evidence that the body does not contain.

## Procedure
1. Identify a real audience question or supported contradiction. Prefer specificity, relevance and useful contrast over exaggeration.
2. Write a headline and opening that make that question visible. Do not promise a hidden secret, universal superiority, scientific proof, scarcity or urgency unless the exact claim is supported and appropriate.
3. Define the promise explicitly in the operator record. Match it to a concrete answer or explanation in the body.
4. Sequence meaningful completions: question, grounded explanation, practical implication. Do not withhold the answer merely to force continued engagement.
5. Preserve scope, qualifications, fees and limits. Make the next action optional and proportionate.
6. Check that the hook, opening, body and CTA tell the same truthful story. The body must actually deliver the payoff, not merely repeat the promise.

## Readable result
Headline; opening and revised passage; short promise-to-payoff check. Keep the recipient-facing passage separate from the check.

## Machine result
headline, opening, body, promise, payoff, call_to_action and claim_refs.
The payoff must be a literal excerpt present in body. This proves textual closure only, not that the answer is useful or true; that still needs review.

## Stop and failure conditions
Clarify if the proposed claim has no supporting content. Refuse false scarcity, manufactured urgency, shame, exploitation of vulnerability or addiction-oriented engagement.
FAIL: “neuroscience proves this works” without evidence; an unanswered cliffhanger; “only three places left” invented to drive a click.
