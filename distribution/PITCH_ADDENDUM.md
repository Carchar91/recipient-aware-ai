Subject: Optional separate angle: an open-source system for checking AI-written requests

I have a separate project update you may find worth considering: Recipient-Aware AI is now available as an experimental open-source prerelease.

It grew from practical work with my personal AI assistant, Seraphim. The question is whether an AI-written message asks for the right thing, includes enough evidence and leaves the recipient a workable next step. The project defines five independently selected operations rather than five personas or an automatic persuasion pipeline.

A visible example uses the same museum-training offer for two readers. One explanation starts with total cost and equipment; the other starts with accuracy and preserving the author's voice. Material terms stay the same. Another example checks an invitation from the recipient's side and inserts supported answers about payment, rights and technical uncertainty.

The inspectable material is the story here: specifications, standalone skills, unchanged synthetic first responses, editorial translations, engineering checks, failures and known limitations. I do not have independent evidence of improved business outcomes or superiority over ordinary prompting. My contribution is the particular operational architecture and its implementation, not the invention of audience awareness.
